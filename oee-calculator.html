What is OEE?

Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE) is a manufacturing KPI that combines three factors: Availability, Performance and Quality. It helps teams describe how effectively equipment is used during planned production time. The result is most useful when the input definitions and time boundaries are consistent across machines, shifts and reporting periods.

OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality
AvailabilityRun time ÷ planned production time
PerformanceIdeal production time ÷ run time
QualityGood units ÷ total units produced

OEE calculation example

Assume planned production time is 450 minutes, unplanned downtime is 45 minutes, ideal cycle time is 30 seconds per unit, total output is 700 units and good output is 680 units.

  • Run time: 450 − 45 = 405 minutes.
  • Availability: 405 ÷ 450 = 90%.
  • Ideal production time: 700 × 30 ÷ 60 = 350 minutes.
  • Performance: 350 ÷ 405 ≈ 86.4%.
  • Quality: 680 ÷ 700 ≈ 97.1%.
  • OEE: 90% × 86.4% × 97.1% ≈ 75.5%.

This is an illustrative calculation using the stated inputs. In actual reporting, confirm the ideal cycle time, planned production time and treatment of planned stops against your plant's approved OEE standard.

How to use the OEE result

Review Availability, Performance and Quality separately before deciding what to improve. Low Availability may point to downtime; low Performance may indicate speed losses or minor stops; low Quality may indicate rejects or defects. These are investigation prompts, not automatic diagnoses—the production data and shop-floor context should confirm the cause.

Common input checks

  • Use consistent units for time and cycle time.
  • Downtime should not exceed planned production time.
  • Good units should not exceed total units produced.
  • If calculated Performance exceeds 100%, verify cycle time, output counts and time boundaries.
  • Apply the same KPI definitions when comparing equipment or periods.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does OEE stand for?

OEE stands for Overall Equipment Effectiveness. It combines Availability, Performance and Quality.

What is a good OEE score?

There is no single target suitable for every factory and process. Compare results with your plant's targets and consistently defined data.

Why can OEE Performance exceed 100%?

Check the ideal cycle time, output count, downtime, planned-time boundary and approved KPI definitions.

What is Overall Equipment Effectiveness (OEE)?

OEE is a manufacturing KPI used to evaluate how effectively equipment is used during planned production time. It combines three factors so teams can distinguish downtime, speed losses and quality losses instead of looking at one number alone.

OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality
01

Availability

Run time divided by planned production time. It reflects the time lost to downtime included in your KPI definition.

Formula: Run Time ÷ Planned Production Time

02

Performance

Ideal production time compared with actual run time. Unexpected speed losses or short stops may reduce this factor.

Formula: Ideal Cycle Time × Total Count ÷ Run Time

03

Quality

The share of total produced units that meet the good-unit definition used by your site.

Formula: Good Count ÷ Total Count

Worked OEE calculation example

Suppose a production line has 450 minutes of planned production time, 45 minutes of downtime, an ideal cycle time of 30 seconds per unit, 700 total units and 680 good units.

  • Planned production time450 min
  • Downtime45 min
  • Run time405 min
  • Ideal cycle time30 sec/unit
  • Total / good output700 / 680
Illustrative OEE result
75.5%

Availability 90.0% × Performance 86.4% × Quality 97.1% ≈ 75.5%

Calculation steps

  • Run time: 450 − 45 = 405 minutes.
  • Availability: 405 ÷ 450 = 90.0%.
  • Ideal production time: 700 × 30 ÷ 60 = 350 minutes.
  • Performance: 350 ÷ 405 ≈ 86.4%.
  • Quality: 680 ÷ 700 ≈ 97.1%.
  • OEE: 0.90 × 0.8642 × 0.9714 ≈ 75.5%.

This example is illustrative. Confirm how your plant defines planned production time, planned stops, ideal cycle time, rework and good units before comparing OEE across lines or reporting periods.

How to interpret your OEE results

Use the three factors to decide where to investigate first. They indicate the type of loss to examine, but they do not identify the root cause by themselves.

Low Availability

Review downtime categories, breakdowns, changeovers and the planned-time boundary.

Low Performance

Check actual speed, minor stops, cycle-time assumptions and production counts.

Low Quality

Review rejects, defects, startup losses and how good units are counted.

Common OEE calculation mistakes

  • Mixing seconds and minutes in cycle-time calculations.
  • Using an ideal cycle time that no longer reflects the process.
  • Counting planned stops inconsistently between machines or shifts.
  • Letting good-unit count exceed total-unit count.
  • Ignoring Performance above 100%, which may indicate mismatched inputs.
  • Comparing lines with different KPI definitions without explaining the difference.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does OEE stand for?

OEE stands for Overall Equipment Effectiveness. It combines Availability, Performance and Quality.

What is the OEE formula?

OEE equals Availability multiplied by Performance multiplied by Quality. Convert each factor to a decimal before multiplying, then express the result as a percentage.

What is a good OEE score?

There is no single target suitable for every factory and process. Compare results with your site's targets, operating context and consistently defined data.

Do I need Power BI to use the free calculator?

No. The free browser-based OEE calculator does not require Power BI. Power BI Desktop is required for the separate premium templates.
